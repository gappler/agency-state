The practice

Inside the source

What a codified brand contains, what it leaves open, and what it never invents.

The inventory

A codified brand expresses the brand as source: structured decisions a machine can read, a person can review, and a system can check against.

The inventory below is the baseline I use for BrandBuilder and assessment work: 46 slots, of which 34 are required, 11 recommended, and 1 optional. Each slot is scored as filled, partial, empty, or not applicable. That turns the work into a gap report instead of an impression.

assessment status: filled: current, sourced, ready to codify partial: exists, but stale, scattered, or unsourced empty: the brand has not decided n/a: this brand does not need it

Identity and architecture

This is the material most brand teams already have in some form: the name, the lines, the values, and the way products or sub-brands relate to the parent brand. The work is making the rules explicit enough for a system to use.

identity: required: - brand name and usage rules - tagline, positioning line, and preview line - what the name means, and what it does not mean recommended: - values as stated in the brand book - mission or purpose, if the brand uses one architecture: recommended: - product and sub-brand naming structure - capitalization rules - names that may never be abbreviated

Positioning and positions

Positioning states where the brand sits: audience, category, differentiators, and the basis for those claims. Positions are different. They are the brand's chosen perspectives, the category debates it takes a side in, and the things it deliberately disagrees with.

Both matter. Positioning keeps the brand legible. Positions give the brand a point of view.

positioning: required: - positioning statement - audience definition - category - differentiators, stated as claims with their basis optional: - named competitor facts, factual and verifiable positions: recommended: - what the brand believes - category debates it takes a side in - what it deliberately disagrees with - owner and date for each position

Voice

Voice needs both rules and examples. Data points give the system checkable habits: preferred words, banned words, punctuation, formatting, reading level, and register shifts. Exemplars give the model whole passages the team agrees sound right.

Voice judgment still belongs to a person. A system can catch banned words and formatting errors. It cannot decide whether a line has the right feel for the brand.

voice: required: - preferred and banned words - sentence-length tendency and reading level - person: first, second, or third - punctuation and formatting rules - style guide of record and house exceptions - 3-5 whole-passage exemplars, kept verbatim recommended: - register splits - channel variations, stated as differences from the core voice

Visual identity

Visual identity belongs in the source when it can be stated as values, files, and rules. Exact values make the visual brand checkable. A system can catch an off-palette color or non-brand typeface before a person reviews the work.

Every brand also builds a website, so spacing, imagery, and iconography are required as well. They are guidance, not checks. The system serves them to whoever builds the page, but whether a layout or an icon fits the brand is a call for a person.

visual: required, checked: - palette with exact values - typefaces and weights - logo usage rules required, guidance: - spacing and layout rules - imagery and illustration direction - iconography

Products and claims

This is where many AI mistakes start. The model names an old product, invents a specification, drops a required condition, or repeats a claim without its proof. Product facts and claims need to sit together so the system knows what may be said and what must travel with it.

products: required: - product list with status: active or retired - facts copy may state - specifications and names - price policy, or a route to a person - approved claims per product - required conditions for each claim

Approved language and stories

Some language should be reused as approved: company descriptions, disclosures, legal language, attributions, and published passages the team already trusts. Stories are stored separately, tagged so the right story can be served for the right job.

approved language: required: - company descriptions at standard lengths - legal and disclosure language - required attributions recommended: - best published passages worth remixing - provenance and usage conditions for each passage stories: recommended: - origin story - named case anecdotes - mistakes the brand learned from - tags for topic, audience, and length

Rules

Rules are the part of the source a check can enforce or route. They need to be specific, sourced, and clear about their basis. A rule can come verbatim from the brand material, be inferred during codification, or be decided by the team during review.

rules: required: - never-say list - claim conditions - formatting rules recommended: - conditional rules with precise conditions each rule records: source: where it came from basis: verbatim, inferred, or decided route: check automatically or send to a person

Decision rights and review

Decision rights name who owns each class of judgment: voice decisions, claim approvals, visual exceptions, legal review, and source updates. When the system cannot settle a question, it needs an address.

The review ledger holds what codification found but could not decide: inferred rules, conflicting sources, stale material, missing proof, and decisions left for the team.

decision rights: required: - owner for voice decisions - owner for claim approvals - owner for visual exceptions - owner for legal review - owner for source updates needs review: required: - inferences made during codification - conflicts found in the source material - stale or unsourced entries - decisions left for a person

What stays out

The source should make gaps visible. If the brand has not decided something, the slot stays empty and routes to a person. The codifier does not invent the answer.

Voice enforcement stays out too. Exemplars and rules can improve generation, and checks can catch specific errors, but voice fit remains a judgment call. The system should route that question to a person.

kept out: invented content: empty slots stay empty brand creation: undecided brands need strategy first voice scoring: judgment routes to a person stale live values: prices and fast-changing facts route to an owner

The output

The useful output is a reviewed source the team can run on: structured brand files, provenance, gaps, conflicts, and the decisions the team needs to make before the source becomes authoritative.

That is why the first Brand OS engagement usually begins with an assessment. The assessment shows what can be codified now, what needs a decision first, and which automations should wait until the source is ready.
