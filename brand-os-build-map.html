The practice

The Brand OS build map

What the system is made of, what already runs, and what gets built around a team's work.

The map

A Brand OS has to turn the brand into source, put that source where AI can use it, check the parts with right answers, route the parts that need judgment, and keep improving as the team works.

Those responsibilities sound like one system, but they are not one job. The brand has to become usable, move through tools, keep judgment with the team, and stay current as the work changes. If those responsibilities blur, AI brand work turns into one vague question: can we make this on brand?

The better question is more specific: which part of the system is responsible here?

01Codified brand: the brand as source a machine can read and a person can govern.
02Infrastructure: the files, ownership, access, and versioning around that source.
03Automations: the agents and workflows that use the brand to make recurring work.
04Enforcement: rule checks for settled decisions and review paths for judgment.
05Connections: the brand served into the tools where the work happens.
06Governance: who decides what ships, what needs review, and what becomes a rule.
07Measurement: signals that make quality, drift, and throughput visible.
08Team adoption: how people learn to direct the system instead of carrying every rule themselves.
09Operating model: how the brand holds across the team, agencies, freelancers, and vendors.

Codify the source

The foundation is codification. A brand book is useful, but it was written for people. It mixes principles, examples, preferences, claims, visual direction, and inherited judgment in a form a person can interpret. AI needs the brand in a form that can be served, checked, updated, and traced.

That is the job of BrandBuilder, Agency State's codification tool. It reads the source material and turns it into a structured brand: voice, visual identity, positioning, architecture, rules, product facts, and a review ledger for anything inferred, conflicting, stale, or undecided.

.brand/ source/ brand.yaml voice.yaml visual.yaml positioning.yaml architecture.yaml rules.yaml products.yaml needs-review.yaml brand-runtime.json interaction-policy.json tokens.json

On Yowie, a demo outdoor-gear brand, BrandBuilder codifies source into a .brand/ structure, verifies provenance, then compiles the reviewed source into three machine contracts: a brand runtime the AI can read, an interaction policy the checks can use, and design tokens for the visual layer.

The discipline is provenance. Every extracted value should keep enough source trail for a person to inspect it before the system treats it as the brand.

attribute: Restrained definition: "No exclamation points. No superlatives without evidence..." source: "brand-book, Voice" basis: verbatim
needs-review: path: rules.claims[retention-names-condition] issue: "The specific condition is inferred..." basis: inferred

Serve it into the tools

Once the brand is source, the next job is serving it. The system can ask for the right part of the brand at the moment the work starts.

The current serving layer is a client-run MCP server. It reads the compiled contracts and exposes them as tools, including context, voice, identity, positioning, never-say rules, interaction policy, visual tokens, and a rule check.

get_context get_voice get_identity get_positioning get_never_say get_interaction_policy get_tokens check_copy(text)

The current layer does a narrower job: the brand can be served from one source into AI tools over MCP, and that served source can include both context and checks. Broader stack integration is a delivery question for each team.

The shared source stops each tool from carrying its own drifting copy of the brand.

Check what has an answer

Some brand decisions have right answers: a banned word, an approved color, a brand font, a product name in the catalog. Those checks should not wait for a senior person.

The rule check reads the compiled interaction policy and tokens, then checks copy or markup against the rules the brand has already settled.

Conquer the backcountry with our ultimate, game-changing pack!

Against Yowie's rules, the check flags the never-say words and the exclamation point. It is the kind of repeated miss a system should catch before a person spends attention on it.

[hard ] never-say Never-say word: 'ultimate'. @33 [hard ] never-say Never-say word: 'game-changing'. @43 [hard ] no-exclamation No exclamation points. Not in copy, not in subject lines, not in social.: found '!'. @61 3 hard, 0 soft/review

Source checks work the same way when the rule is clean: a product appears in the catalog, or a claim points back to the file. The system should stop where the evidence stops. Whether a claim is worth making, the voice lands, or an image feels right still belongs to judgment.

Route judgment

The useful split is settled versus unsettled. Machines are useful where the brand has already made the decision and the rule can be checked. People are needed where the decision is contextual: a fair comparison, enough proof, a brief worth answering, work worth making.

Governance gives the system its decision rights. Someone has to own what ships, what needs review, what becomes a rule, and what stays a human decision.

A simple routing example: if a draft uses a banned phrase, the system can block it. If a draft makes a new performance comparison, the system should send it to the person who owns claims. If that same comparison becomes repeatable and approved, it can move from judgment into source or a rule.

This part becomes real in use, with teams, deadlines, and authority. AI can increase production before an organization has decided how review should work. Without governance, judgment gets buried under volume.

Measure and improve

The system also needs feedback. The useful signals show what is breaking, what is getting caught, and what should become source or a rule.

I have run measurement work around rule checks, source checks, and verification, including a 241-draft verification run on Yowie. The practical lesson is that measurement has to stay close to the rule being measured. A source check can be useful while a spec check may still misread a shipping fee or rounded comparison price.

Measurement earns trust when it improves the review path. A repeated correction may mean the source is missing, the rule is too vague, the check is not reliable yet, or the decision belongs with a person. Repeated friction becomes a better source, a better rule, or a clearer route.

What the map is for

The map gives a buyer or builder better questions than "do we have an AI tool?"

  1. SourceWhat does the AI read before it works, and who owns it?
  2. EvidenceWhich claims, facts, and rules can point back to a source?
  3. ChecksWhat can the system verify reliably, and what should it refuse to judge?
  4. RoutesWho gets the work when the machine cannot settle the decision?
  5. CorrectionsHow do repeated fixes become better source, rules, or review paths?

The build map shows what the system is made of, what already runs, and what gets built around a team's actual work.
