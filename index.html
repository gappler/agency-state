Marketing teams now use AI in more than 20% of their work. Agencies and brand teams still answer for what ships. I help them put AI to work at the standard their clients expect.

Agency State

The system I build with them is Brand OS. It runs inside Claude Code and Codex workflows, serves a single brand source to automations and agents, checks the work against that source, and improves as it works with the team in production.

Recent research found that AI models, given a brand's voice to imitate, still write like AI and can't see the difference. With Brand OS, automations and agents draft and adapt the team's recurring marketing work from the brand source. Checks catch specific errors, and because the model can't judge its own voice, people review each draft and make the brand decisions. When the work reveals a gap, the team approves changes to the source, checks, or review process. I start by assessing the existing brand material, the decisions to make, and which work to build first.

Background

Most recently, during a decade at The American Institute of Architects as Managing Director of Brand & Strategic Marketing, I led record-breaking membership and conference campaigns, modernized marketing operations, and built a 12-person team. I managed the development of AIAU, the AIA's first on-demand continuing education platform. I was also the business owner for a brand redesign adopted across the national organization and its chapter network, with visual identity by Pentagram. Before the AIA, I spent six years as VP of Digital Marketing at GRAFIK, an agency in Alexandria, Virginia, on B2B clients including Autodesk and Accenture. Earlier, I led US digital marketing for Nissan and Infiniti at Designory, worked on Lincoln Mercury at Young & Rubicam, and was in-house at Bacardi Global Brands. I've worked agency-side or in-house on brand clients my whole career.

I'm a builder at heart, and for the past two years I've been building with Claude Code, Codex, and other automation tools. I'm developing Agency State's BrandBuilder to turn a brand's written and visual guidelines into a source AI can work from. As is often the case, it's a bigger problem than I thought, but I'm testing it on demo brands and it's nearly ready for client work.

I think much of an agency's time goes to the work between its predictable workflows, such as selling an idea, explaining it, gathering feedback, and getting approval. I'm building a campaign engine for that work. Working from a strong brief, agents prepare whatever material moves a campaign from one phase to the next, and a person approves it before the next phase starts.
