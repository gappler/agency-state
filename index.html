Inside Brand OS
01

Source

The codified brand: the team's decided material turned into structured source, with provenance, gaps, conflicts, and open decisions made visible.

02

Production

Automations and agents make the team's recurring work: drafts, adaptations, and channel versions. The brief and the idea come from the team; the source supplies the brand side.

03

Control

Checks test what a machine can verify, such as product facts, claim conditions, banned words, and visual rules. What needs judgment routes to a person.

04

Feedback

Corrections return to the source, the rules, or the review path, so repeated catches become settled guidance instead of the same review burden returning.

05

Operators

The people who own the brand's decisions keep the system current: approving exceptions, retiring old facts, settling open questions, and deciding what can ship.

The first build

Agency State's BrandBuilder is the codification tool I'm building first. It reads a team's existing brand material, shows what is ready to codify, and produces structured brand source for the team to review. I'm testing it on demo brands now.

Its output is reviewed source the Brand OS can run on: structured brand files with provenance, plus the gaps, conflicts, and decisions the team settles before the source becomes authoritative.

Building isn't new to me. I used to write code, building CD-ROM games, presentations, and kiosks; for the past two years I've built with Claude Code, and more recently Codex. I published a guide to Claude Code for marketing teams, built a campaign engine in which agents run a campaign through phases a person approves, and have a few AI products of my own in the works. Context is not control shows what one of my tests found.
